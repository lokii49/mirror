Design review · 21 October 2026 · for Lokesh · nothing here is built yet

MirrorNotes, three surfaces, one system.

What the colour and theme do to a reader, what they don't yet do, and the layout changes that take the iOS and Mac apps toward 10/10. Every mockup below uses the live website's components, which already mirror the app's tokens. Sample journal only.

1. Colour and theme, read psychologically

What the palette is doing right

  • Lavender paper + ink. Low-saturation, slightly cool paper lowers arousal. Journaling needs a low-arousal state to be honest; a white or warm-cream page would feel like a form or a notebook you perform in. The tint says "evening, private, unhurried" before a word is read.
  • Violet as the only accent. Violet sits between red (urgency) and blue (trust). It reads as introspective and slightly premium without being clinical. One accent means the eye always knows what is interactive.
  • Mood colours are the only loud colour. Everything else is quiet, so the day tiles, rails and pills carry the emotional information. The hook: colour on this screen means you. It makes the week strip and the timeline readable in a glance, which is the daily reward loop.
  • New York serif for what the app says back. A serif in a sea of sans marks a different speaker. The reflection reads as a voice, not a UI label. That is the trust mechanism behind "You wrote, …".
  • Ember, three uses. Scarcity of warmth works: the paywall, the mood alert and the first reflection are the three moments that deserve a heartbeat.

Where it under-delivers

  • Lavender is the wellness category's colour. Calm, Headspace-adjacent apps live here. The distinctiveness has to come from the components (tiles, rails, quotes), not the hue. That is why the website leans on those, and why the apps should too. Keep the hue.
  • Joy and anxiety are the same colour. Joyful #FFD54F and Anxious #FFC107 are both yellow; Energized and Frustrated are both orange. In the week strip a good day and an anxious day look alike. Valence should be legible before the label is.
  • Dark mode has no depth. Card #110E1C on page #08060F is a 1.3:1 step; cards float only by their border. Light mode gets paper-on-desk; dark gets ink-on-ink.
  • Insights mixes three card grammars. Today, This Week's Mood and the Weekly Digest each have a different header, chip and button style. The reader re-learns the page every scroll.
  • Violet does too many jobs in the apps. Accent text, tint backgrounds, selected states, CTAs. The website now separates "accent text" from "button fill" (a slightly deeper violet for buttons); the apps should too.

Proposal A: encode valence in saturation

Positive moods stay bright and saturated. Difficult moods become dusty versions of their family: same hue memory, lower chroma. The brain reads saturation as energy and warmth as pleasantness; a muted amber for Anxious says "uneasy", a bright yellow for Joyful says "light". The twelve stay distinct for the colour-blind reader because hue spacing is unchanged; only the difficult six lose chroma. Token-only change in MirrorTheme.moodColor, flows to widgets and the website.

What the palette validator measured

Run on all 66 pairs of the twelve (dataviz validator, light surface). Current: worst normal-vision pair Anxious↔Joyful ΔE 4.9, worst colour-blind pair Drained↔Content ΔE 2.1 (deutan). Proposal A: worst normal-vision pair Anxious↔Energized ΔE 7.4, worst colour-blind pair Drained↔Content ΔE 2.9. Four further retunings did not move the colour-blind floor; the twelve-category limit is the cause, not the values.

So the honest version of Proposal A is two parts. (1) The dusted six, which fix the one confusion a sighted reader hits daily, joy against anxiety. (2) A second cue for valence wherever colour stands alone: in the week strip and the Mood Timeline a difficult day gets a thin ink ring on its tile or dot; pills and lists already carry the name. Colour-blind readers then read good/hard without the hue. Both land in step 1.

Difficult six: Anxious #D4A84B, Overwhelmed #D96A63, Frustrated #D98A4F, Drained #9A96A3, Sad #6A9FD1, Numb #C3CBD1. The `moodOnColorText` dark-text set gets re-derived for the new values and likely grows to Sad, Drained and Frustrated (white on those lands near 2.8:1; current Sad and Drained already fail with white). `moodScoreColor`'s anchors are Overwhelmed, Frustrated and Numb, three of the six, so the aggregate ramp moves in the same commit. Sentinel mode's ember accent and HUD are untouched by this change. Validator results below.

2. iOS: three layout changes

The iOS app is the source of the system and already the strongest surface. These three changes remove the places where the design works against its own content.

2a. Insights, one card grammar

Every card: icon + name + time in the header, a hairline, serif body, one capsule action. Only Today wears the accent ring. The collapsed Past reflections row stays as it is. This Week's Mood keeps its dots in mood colour; nothing else is coloured. The two other Today states keep the same header row: the scheduled card ("Reflection generates at 7:00 PM") becomes serif body + a quiet moon glyph in the header's time slot; the digest's "write 3 entries this week for a fresh one" line moves into the digest card's header as its time text.

6. Five decisions

Mood colours. Dust the six difficult moods and add the valence ring (Proposal A, both parts), or keep the current twelve? Recommendation: both parts; the validator shows colour alone can't separate twelve moods either way, so the ring is what makes the week strip honest.
Decide
Brain View default. 2D with labels as the default and 3D one tap away, or keep 3D as the landing view? Recommendation: 2D default; 3D stays the showpiece one tap away.
Decide
Mac detail pane. Entry + its reflection + related entries (3a), or entry + reflection only? Recommendation: 3a; related entries are the one thing a big screen can do that the phone can't.
Decide
Entries list typography. Move the per-entry font (serif, mono, from the 3.1.3 Aa panel) out of the list row and into the entry only, so the list reads in one voice? Recommendation: yes; the font still shows where the writing is.
Decide
Which build. Tokens, Brain View, Entries and the Mac changes on 3.1.5 now (no gating, fast TestFlight), merged into 3.1.6 after. The Insights card grammar touches the same files 3.1.6 is rewriting for saved-or-lock, so it goes straight onto 3.1.6 to avoid a merge fight. Recommendation: exactly that split, so design and paywall don't land in the same week and nothing gets merged twice. Pushing 3.1.5 starts Xcode Cloud and the TestFlight group automatically; that push waits for your word.
Decide
